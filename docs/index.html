Why I built this

I'm a dad. I was losing my son to YouTube.

Not to the good stuff. To the other stuff. Minecraft "secret room" videos. "Noob vs Pro." Kid vloggers building treehouses for 40 minutes. One video bled into the next. Autoplay did the work, and by the time I looked up, the whole afternoon was gone.

Why every screen-time app you've tried has failed

Your kid is stuck in the Autoplay Loop.

It isn't a willpower problem, his or yours. It's a design problem. YouTube is built to never end, and most parental controls only know one move: off.

STEP 1

The feed never ends

The algorithm serves flashy, no-value videos on autoplay, each one picked to keep him watching the next. There's even a word for it now: "brain rot," Oxford's 2024 Word of the Year.4

STEP 2

Blockers start a war

All-or-nothing limits can't tell Science Max from a prank channel. So kids fight them, or find the workaround everyone's sharing online.5 The fight becomes the routine.

STEP 3

Then nothing fills the gap

When the screen finally goes off, he's bored, and boredom sends him right back to the screen. The loop restarts tomorrow.

The research points the same way: it's the loop, not the minutes.

A 2025 JAMA study followed more than 4,000 kids. Total screen time didn't predict their mental-health problems. Addictive patterns of use did, with roughly double the risk of suicidal behaviors.6 Counting minutes was never going to fix this. You have to break the pattern.

The fix

Tube Timer runs the Get-Up Loop instead.

Four simple moves that turn YouTube back into a tool. No daily fights, because the app is the bad guy, not you.

🧠

1. Sort

Science, space, math, reading, art, crafts, quizzes and music are always OK. Passive brain-rot gets a small daily allowance. Inappropriate stuff is blocked.

⏯

2. Ration

A daily limit and a small "fun time" budget, counted only while a video is actually playing. Paused or searching doesn't count.

🕺

3. Move

After a sitting, the screen becomes a dance party with dancing chickens: get up, go outside, talk to people, shake your butt. It can't be clicked away.

🏆

4. Earn

Want more fun time? Watch a short math or science video and answer 5 questions. Pass, and you get +20 minutes. Learning becomes the currency.

When a sitting is over, YouTube closes and the dance party takes over the screen until the break ends. Kids can't click it away.

Questions parents ask

FAQ

Does it work on Windows, iPad or phones?

Right now Tube Timer runs on Macs (macOS 13 Ventura or newer) and watches YouTube in Chrome, Safari, Brave, Edge and Arc. A mobile version is coming soon. If you buy the Mac download, you'll get it free. Windows is on the roadmap.

Will my kid just get around it?

The breaks and blocks take over the whole screen and can't be clicked away, and every setting is behind your PIN. For the strongest setup, give your child a Standard (non-admin) macOS account, so they can't quit or delete apps.

Does it block all of YouTube?

No. That's the point. Learning videos (science, space, math, reading, art, crafts, quizzes, music) stay open. Pure "brain-rot" gets a small daily fun-time budget you control. Only inappropriate stuff is blocked outright. You can always-allow or always-block specific channels.

How does it know what's a learning video?

It reads each video's public YouTube info (title, channel, category, family-safe flag) and applies smart rules. A clear learning angle like "quiz," "science" or "how to draw" wins even on a gaming channel. You can add an optional AI key so Claude judges tricky videos by your rules.

Is my family's data collected?

No. There are no accounts and no tracking. Everything runs and stays on your Mac.

Is there a free version?

Yes. Tube Timer's source code is public on GitHub for technical parents who want to build it themselves with Terminal. The paid download is the easy, ready-to-run version, and it supports a dad building more of these tools.

Can I customize the times, limits and videos?

Everything: daily limit, sitting length, break length, fun-time minutes, weekend rules, fun-time hours, YouTube bedtime, allowed categories and channels, the suggested videos, and the earn-time quizzes.

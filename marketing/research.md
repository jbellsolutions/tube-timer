**Positioning:** the existing tools either **block** (and start a fight) or **monitor** (and leave the habit alone). None of them know the difference between a Science Max episode and a "secret room" video, and none of them make the kid **get up and move**. Tube Timer is the only one built around the *pattern*: the autoplay loop.

## Big idea (Todd Brown–style)

**Big idea:** *Screen-time limits fail because they count the wrong thing. It's not how many minutes, it's the loop.*
The 2025 JAMA study backs this: total screen time didn't predict harm, but addictive patterns did.

**Mechanism of the problem: "The Autoplay Loop"**
1. The algorithm serves endless, flashy, no-value videos ("brain rot") on autoplay.
2. Timers and blockers are all-or-nothing, so kids fight them, find workarounds, or switch to another video.
3. When the screen goes off, nothing fills the gap. Kids are bored, so they beg to go back on.

**Mechanism of the solution: "The Get-Up Loop" (Sort → Ration → Move → Earn)**
1. **Sort:** learning, music and creativity are free; brain-rot is rationed; inappropriate content is blocked.
2. **Ration:** a small daily "fun time," counted only while a video is actually playing.
3. **Move:** a forced dance-party break (get up, go outside, talk to people).
4. **Earn:** more fun time by learning (watch a short math or science video, answer 5 questions).

**Emotional drivers:** guilt ("I use it as a babysitter"), helplessness ("every app I try, he gets around"), fear (mental-health headlines), the daily fight, the "now what?" boredom gap, and hope (the kid who *does* get up, play piano, and go outside when the screen stops).
